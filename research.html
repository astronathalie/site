Research

My current research focuses on variable stars such as cataclysmic variables, using multiwavelength observations and small telescopes. I also study star formation and chemical enrichment in low-mass galaxies.

Research areas

Cataclysmic variables

My current research focuses on variable stars such as cataclysmic variables. Specifically, I am interested in multiwavelength studies, which can reveal information about the interaction and magnetic fields in these objects.

Recent work with the MACRO Consortium pairs simultaneous optical and radio observations of magnetic cataclysmic variables.

Be stars

I am involved in projects studying Be stars, and am specifically interested in what studying their spectra over time can reveal about the evolution of their decretion disks.

Low-mass galaxies

In addition to work on stars, I have studied extragalactic star formation, chemical enrichment, and galaxy evolution, specifically in low-mass galaxies.

I am particularly interested in understanding how chemical enrichment has taken place over cosmic time in the lowest mass systems.

Facilities

Knox College Observatory

I serve as the Director of the Knox College Observatory, which houses a 17-inch PlaneWave CDK telescope. This facility supports student research, educational activities, and public outreach.

MACRO Consortium

I am the Director of the MACRO Consortium, an active science collaboration that collectively operates the robotic Robert L. Mutel Telescope, a 20-inch telescope with novel instrumentation in Sonoita, AZ.

See my publications for results from this work.
